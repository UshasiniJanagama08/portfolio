Engineering decisions

Incremental loads, not full reloadsOnly new and changed rows move, so runs stay short as the catalog grows.
Staging, then MERGEA failed or repeated run can be rerun safely without creating duplicate rows.

Retries and alertsTemporary failures recover on their own, and real failures notify someone right away.

SnowflakedbtPythonscikit-learnTableau

  • Applied TF-IDF and NMF topic modeling to 104,615 narratives, producing 8 business themes.

What are credit card customers complaining about, is it getting worse, and how do card issuers compare?

What I built

  • Snowflake RAW, STAGING, and ANALYTICS layers, reconciled on row counts, distinct IDs, and date ranges.
  • 15 rule-based quality checks logged to a table that feeds a scorecard dashboard.
  • dbt star schema with one fact table and five dimensions.
  • Role-based access, dynamic masking on narratives and ZIP codes, PII tags, metric definitions, and a dbt lineage graph.

Problems I solved

  • The CFPB stopped publishing narratives mid-project. I recovered 106,344 from its archive, matched by complaint ID with zero gaps.
  • 7.8% of narratives were copied form letters creating fake themes. I detected and separated them by matching shared opening and closing text.
  • dbt rebuilds dropped security policies, so I added a post-hook that re-applies masking on every run.
  • Discover's complaints moved under Capital One in April 2026, which inflated Capital One's growth until I combined the two.

Key findings

  • Unresolved-dispute complaints grew 84.6% in a year, confirmed by the topic model.
  • An apparent 42% drop in credit reporting complaints came entirely from credit bureaus (down 80.8%). Against card issuers they rose 46.4%.
  • Three companies caused 65.5% of late responses over eight months.
  • Issue-by-issue comparison showed issuers' relief-rate gap was about half the raw figure.
  • Disputes and fees drive about 70% of monetary relief.

Machine Learning · Data Engineering

AI Data Engineering Assistant

Classifies failed Spark and Airflow pipeline runs, finds similar past incidents, and recommends fixes grounded in those incidents and operational runbooks.

PythonFastAPIFAISSLlama 3scikit-learnStreamlit
  • Built a hybrid rules + ML classifier for 14 failure types, reaching 95% accuracy on a time-split test set and lifting accuracy on unseen error messages from 36% to 79%.
  • Built retrieval over 2,400 prior incidents and 14 runbooks, producing fix recommendations that cite the incidents and runbook sections they came from.

When a pipeline fails at 2 a.m., what broke, has it happened before, and what fixed it last time?

What I built

  • A log parser that strips timestamps, masks hosts, IDs, and paths, drops harmless warnings, and keeps only error lines plus a stable failure signature.
  • A classifier combining regex rules for known error signatures with a TF-IDF and logistic regression model, flagging low-confidence cases for review.
  • Similarity search over past incidents and runbook sections, which pins the predicted failure type's diagnosis, fix, and prevention guidance.
  • A dataset generator producing 3,000 synthetic incidents modeled on real Spark, YARN, Delta Lake, Kafka, and Airflow error messages, each with a root cause and fix.
  • Tests covering parsing, classification, retrieval, and end-to-end recommendations.

Problems I solved

  • My first version scored 100% accuracy, meaning the test was too easy. I added compound failures, misleading warnings, truncated logs, 2% label noise, and a time-based split until the numbers meant something.
  • An out-of-memory error often surfaces later as a shuffle failure. The model learned to label by root cause, not the last error in the log.
  • The ML model fell to 36% on error messages it had never seen. Adding signature rules raised that to 79%.
  • Every recommendation's citations are checked against the sources actually retrieved, and anything else is flagged as unsupported.

Key results

  • 95% classification accuracy on the 600 most recent incidents, against 90% for rules alone.
  • 99% accuracy on the 95% of cases where the classifier was confident; the remaining 5% are flagged for a human.
  • On error messages withheld from training entirely, the hybrid held 79% accuracy while the model alone fell to 36%.
  • On truncated logs with only the last few lines, accuracy was 66%, the clearest limit of the current approach.
3,000 incidents · 14 failure types · 95% accuracy
